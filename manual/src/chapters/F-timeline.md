---
title: Timeline of Space Exploration and Engineering
abstract: A chronology of the milestones described in this manual — from the theoretical foundations of rocketry and the first liquid-fuelled rocket, through the Space Race, the Shuttle and station era, and the rise of commercial spaceflight, to the events of 2025 and 2026 — followed by the missions planned for 2026–2035 as of October 2026.
---

Dates are in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) where the precise day matters; a launch late in the evening in the Americas can fall on the next UTC day. "First" claims refer to the first *successful* achievement. Planned dates in the final table are those announced by the responsible agencies or companies as of October 2026; experience suggests that many will slip.

## Foundations (1903–1956)

::: small
| Date | Event |
|----|--------------------|
| 1903 | Konstantin Tsiolkovsky publishes *Exploration of Outer Space by Means of Reaction Devices*, deriving the rocket equation |
| 1919 | Robert Goddard publishes *A Method of Reaching Extreme Altitudes* |
| 1923 | Hermann Oberth publishes *Die Rakete zu den Planetenräumen* (The Rocket into Planetary Space) |
| 16 Mar 1926 | Goddard launches the first liquid-propellant rocket, at Auburn, Massachusetts — a 2.5 s flight to 12.5 m |
| 17 Aug 1933 | GIRD-09, the first Soviet rocket with a liquid oxidiser, flies near Moscow |
| 20 Jun 1944 | A German V-2 (A4) test rocket reaches about 175 km — the first human-made object in space |
| 24 Oct 1946 | A camera on a captured V-2 launched from White Sands takes the first photographs of Earth from space (105 km) |
| 24 Feb 1949 | The two-stage Bumper-WAC reaches 393 km |
| Jul–Aug 1955 | The United States and the Soviet Union announce plans to launch satellites for the International Geophysical Year |
:::

::: key
**Key takeaways**

- It took 54 years from Tsiolkovsky's rocket equation (1903) to Sputnik (1957), and only 12 more to a human landing on the Moon.
- Most "firsts" of robotic planetary exploration came between 1959 and 1976; the decades since have brought orbiters, rovers and sample returns.
- Since 2015 reusable rockets and commercial spacecraft have transformed launch rates: 330 orbital launch attempts in 2025, compared with fewer than 100 a year in 2000–2017.
- 2026 brought the first crewed lunar flight in 54 years, the first orbital flight of Starship and the launch of the Roman Space Telescope.
:::
