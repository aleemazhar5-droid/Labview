# Chapter 32 — Space Stations and Habitats: currency notes (post-2023 facts; checked 18 Oct 2026)

## ISS status
- ISS facts (NASA, current page): mass 419 725 kg; pressurised volume 1 005 m³; habitable 388 m³ (excluding visiting
  vehicles); end-to-end 109 m; truss 94 m; 8 original solar array wings 75–90 kW (old figure); up to 8 spacecraft docked
  at once; >50 computers, ~350 000 sensors, >1.5 M lines of flight code; Canadarm2 17.6 m (57.7 ft), 7 joints. —
  https://www.nasa.gov/international-space-station/space-station-facts-and-figures/ (checked 18 Oct 2026)
- 25 years of continuous occupation on 2 Nov 2025; >290 people from 26 countries have visited (AP/NASA, ISS National Lab). —
  https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/international-space-station-marks-25-years-of-nonstop-human-presence-in-orbit ;
  https://issnationallab.org/?p=50759
- iROSA: original 8 wings degraded to ~160 kW; six iROSAs (each > 20 kW) installed 2021–23 → ~215 kW (ESA/NASA, "20–30 %
  more power"); seventh/final mount (power channel 3B) installed on EVA 6 Aug 2026 (Meir, Menon); final pair of iROSAs
  launched in trunk of CRS-35 (Falcon 9, 13 Oct 2026, Dragon C211, docking Harmony zenith ~15 Oct). —
  https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2021/06/New_solar_arrays_for_the_International_Space_Station ;
  https://www.space.com/space-exploration/international-space-station/spacex-crs-35-cargo-launch-international-space-station ;
  https://spaceflightnow.com/?p=74515
- Canadarm2 joint 5 (wrist) failed 27 May 2026 (elevated motor current); replaced on EVA 30 Jun 2026 (Williams, Meir; 7 h 20 min),
  the arm's fourth repair; failed joint returned on CRS-35 Dragon. — https://spaceq.ca/nasa-astronauts-restore-canadarm2-functionality-during-complex-spacewalk/
- PrK leak: known since 2019; Feb 2024 2.4 lb/day, April 2024 3.7 lb/day (~1.7 kg/day, highest), rated 5×5 risk (NASA OIG,
  Sept 2024); hatch kept closed. Jan 2026 leaks reported stopped after sealant; returned May 2026; 1 Jun 2026 rate rose from
  ~0.45 to ~0.9 kg/day; 5 Jun 2026 five crew sheltered in Crew Dragon for < 2 h; Roscosmos fixed one of two leaks, paused
  structural repair. No later public status found → "unresolved as of mid-2026". —
  https://spacenews.com/?p=506503 ; https://earthsky.org/space/iss-leak-astronauts-briefly-shelter-updates/ ;
  https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/five-iss-crew-members-temporarily-take-shelter-due-to-russian-segment-air-leak/
- Crew-11 early return (first ISS medical evacuation) Jan 2026; Crew-12 (Freedom) splashdown 8 Oct 2026; Crew-13 launched
  1 Oct 2026; Soyuz MS-29 launched 14 Jul 2026 (see ch31 notes). — https://spaceflightnow.com/?p=74515
- ISS cost to NASA: ~$3 B/yr (OIG 2021, IG-22-005); ~$4.1 B/yr incl. related ops & research, 16 % of NASA budget (OIG 2024,
  IG-24-020). — https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/ig-24-020.pdf
- Propellant: ~7 000 kg/yr average for altitude maintenance, debris avoidance and attitude (Wikipedia ISS Propulsion Module,
  citing NASA). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_Propulsion_Module
- Dragon reboost: CRS-31 C208 first Dragon reboost 8 Nov 2024 (Dracos, 12.5 min); CRS-33 (Aug 2025) carried trunk "boost kit"
  with two Dracos. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_CRS-31 ; https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/04/spacex_dragon_iss_reboost/
- Deorbit: USDV (SpaceX, June 2024, up to $843 M; total incl. launch ~ $1.5 B per NASA); Project-Level Design Review
  Nov 2025; > 30 t, needs heavier launcher; launch ~2029; ISS operations to 2030, deorbit early 2031 into South Pacific.
  Roscosmos (Bakanov, TASS 6 Apr 2026): Russia winds down from 2028, deorbit completed 2030. —
  https://spacepolicyonline.com/?p=72843 ; https://tass.com/science/2112209 ;
  https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2025/11/12/what-is-the-us-deorbit-vehicle/

  (29 Apr 2021), Wentian 23.2 t (24 Jul 2022), Mengtian ~23 t (31 Oct 2022); 4 Hall thrusters; 12 CMGs; continuously crewed
  since June 2022. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_space_station

  experiment modules → "double-T", six modules, ~180 t; no dates announced. — https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/china-plans-to-double-the-size-of-its-tiangong-space-station-while-the-iss-nears-its-end ;

  one member to stay ~1 year; Xuntian telescope launch 2027 (LM-5B). — KeepTrack (above); ch31 notes
