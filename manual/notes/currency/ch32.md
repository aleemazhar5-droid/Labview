# Chapter 32 — Space Stations and Habitats: currency notes (post-2023 facts; checked 18 Oct 2026)

## ISS status
- ISS facts (NASA, current page): mass 419 725 kg; pressurised volume 1 005 m³; habitable 388 m³ (excluding visiting
  vehicles); end-to-end 109 m; truss 94 m; 8 original solar array wings 75–90 kW (old figure); up to 8 spacecraft docked
  at once; >50 computers, ~350 000 sensors, >1.5 M lines of flight code; Canadarm2 17.6 m (57.7 ft), 7 joints. —
  https://www.nasa.gov/international-space-station/space-station-facts-and-figures/ (checked 18 Oct 2026)
- 25 years of continuous occupation on 2 Nov 2025; >290 people from 26 countries have visited (AP/NASA, ISS National Lab). —
  https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/international-space-station-marks-25-years-of-nonstop-human-presence-in-orbit ;
  https://issnationallab.org/?p=50759
- iROSA: original 8 wings degraded to ~160 kW; six iROSAs (each > 20 kW) installed 2021–23 → ~215 kW (ESA/NASA, "20–30 %
  more power"); seventh/final mount (power channel 3B) installed on EVA 6 Aug 2026 (Meir, Menon); final pair of iROSAs
  launched in trunk of CRS-35 (Falcon 9, 13 Oct 2026, Dragon C211, docking Harmony zenith ~15 Oct). —
  https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2021/06/New_solar_arrays_for_the_International_Space_Station ;
  https://www.space.com/space-exploration/international-space-station/spacex-crs-35-cargo-launch-international-space-station ;
  https://spaceflightnow.com/?p=74515
- Canadarm2 joint 5 (wrist) failed 27 May 2026 (elevated motor current); replaced on EVA 30 Jun 2026 (Williams, Meir; 7 h 20 min),
  the arm's fourth repair; failed joint returned on CRS-35 Dragon. — https://spaceq.ca/nasa-astronauts-restore-canadarm2-functionality-during-complex-spacewalk/
- PrK leak: known since 2019; Feb 2024 2.4 lb/day, April 2024 3.7 lb/day (~1.7 kg/day, highest), rated 5×5 risk (NASA OIG,
  Sept 2024); hatch kept closed. Jan 2026 leaks reported stopped after sealant; returned May 2026; 1 Jun 2026 rate rose from
  ~0.45 to ~0.9 kg/day; 5 Jun 2026 five crew sheltered in Crew Dragon for < 2 h; Roscosmos fixed one of two leaks, paused
  structural repair. No later public status found → "unresolved as of mid-2026". —
  https://spacenews.com/?p=506503 ; https://earthsky.org/space/iss-leak-astronauts-briefly-shelter-updates/ ;
  https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/five-iss-crew-members-temporarily-take-shelter-due-to-russian-segment-air-leak/
- Crew-11 early return (first ISS medical evacuation) Jan 2026; Crew-12 (Freedom) splashdown 8 Oct 2026; Crew-13 launched
  1 Oct 2026; Soyuz MS-29 launched 14 Jul 2026 (see ch31 notes). — https://spaceflightnow.com/?p=74515
- ISS cost to NASA: ~$3 B/yr (OIG 2021, IG-22-005); ~$4.1 B/yr incl. related ops & research, 16 % of NASA budget (OIG 2024,
  IG-24-020). — https://oig.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/ig-24-020.pdf
- Propellant: ~7 000 kg/yr average for altitude maintenance, debris avoidance and attitude (Wikipedia ISS Propulsion Module,
  citing NASA). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS_Propulsion_Module
- Dragon reboost: CRS-31 C208 first Dragon reboost 8 Nov 2024 (Dracos, 12.5 min); CRS-33 (Aug 2025) carried trunk "boost kit"
  with two Dracos. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_CRS-31 ; https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/04/spacex_dragon_iss_reboost/
- Deorbit: USDV (SpaceX, June 2024, up to $843 M; total incl. launch ~ $1.5 B per NASA); Project-Level Design Review
  Nov 2025; > 30 t, needs heavier launcher; launch ~2029; ISS operations to 2030, deorbit early 2031 into South Pacific.
  Roscosmos (Bakanov, TASS 6 Apr 2026): Russia winds down from 2028, deorbit completed 2030. —
  https://spacepolicyonline.com/?p=72843 ; https://tass.com/science/2112209 ;
  https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2025/11/12/what-is-the-us-deorbit-vehicle/

## Cargo vehicles
- Cygnus XL first flight NG-23, Falcon 9, 14 Sep 2025; ~5 000 kg, 36 m³ (+33 %); berthed 18 Sep after main-engine early
  cut-offs. — https://spaceflightnow.com/2025/09/15/northrop-grummans-1st-cygnus-xl-spacecraft-launches-on-cargo-run-to-the-space-station/
- HTV-X1: H3 F7, Tanegashima, 26 Oct 2025; captured by Canadarm2 (Kimiya Yui) 29 Oct UTC; berthed Harmony nadir 30 Oct;
  ~12 800 lb cargo (NASA caption); unberthed ~5 Mar 2026. Design 4 070 kg pressurised + 1 750 kg unpressurised; 6 months
  berthed + up to 1.5 years free flight. — https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2025/10/20251030-1_e.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTV-X
- Progress MS: 7 440 kg; up to ~2 600 kg payload (≤ 1 800 kg dry, ≤ 1 700 kg refuel propellant, 420 kg water). Progress MS-33
  launched 22 Mar 2026 after Site 31 repair. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_MS
- Dream Chaser: free-flyer demo only (no ISS berthing) NET late 2026 on Vulcan (Sept 2025 contract change); ~5 500 kg up,
  ~1 850 kg return (sources vary). — currency_2026.md ; https://spacepolicyonline.com/?p=84158
- Tianzhou-10: 11 May 2026, LM-7, ~6.2 t cargo + ~700 kg propellant. — https://keeptrack.space/deep-dive/tiangong-space-station-2026

## Tiangong
- ~100 t (Wikipedia, 2024) / ~90 t (CMSA, KeepTrack); 340 m³ pressurised, 122 m³ habitable; ~390 km, 41.5°; Tianhe 22.5 t
  (29 Apr 2021), Wentian 23.2 t (24 Jul 2022), Mengtian ~23 t (31 Oct 2022); 4 Hall thrusters; 12 CMGs; continuously crewed
  since June 2022. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_space_station
- Expansion (CMSA, 22 Jun 2026): new ~20 t multifunctional module with six docking ports on Tianhe → cross shape; then two more
  experiment modules → "double-T", six modules, ~180 t; no dates announced. — https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/china-plans-to-double-the-size-of-its-tiangong-space-station-while-the-iss-nears-its-end ;
  https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3352049/chinas-tiangong-space-station-double-size-nasa-phases-out-iss
- Shenzhou-20 window crack (Nov 2025), Shenzhou-22 launched uncrewed as lifeboat 25 Nov 2025; Shenzhou-23 launched 24 May 2026,
  one member to stay ~1 year; Xuntian telescope launch 2027 (LM-5B). — KeepTrack (above); ch31 notes

## Commercial and national successors
- CLD history: Phase 1 SAAs Dec 2021 ($415.6 M: Blue Origin $130 M, Nanoracks/Starlab $160 M, Northrop $125.6 M).
  Aug 2025 Duffy directive → funded SAAs, reduced minimum capability; 24 Mar 2026 NASA floated government module on ISS;
  1 Jun 2026 reverted to commercial plan; draft RFP July 2026 (FFP multi-award IDIQ, crewed flight test by 2029); final RFP
  9 Oct 2026 (RELEASE26-086), proposals due 8 Dec 2026, "two or more" awards spring 2027. —
  https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-seeks-us-industry-plans-for-commercial-space-stations/ ;
  https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/nasa-agrees-with-industry-keep-cld-program-as-is/ ;
  https://www.executivegov.com/articles/jared-isaacman-nasa-administrator-cldc-program-draft-rfp ;
  https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319946/20260708/commercial-space-station-race-nasa-draft-rfp-sets-2029-crewed-flight-deadline.htm
- Vast: Haven Demo flew Nov 2025; Haven-1 slipped (20 Jan 2026) to NET Q1 2027 on Falcon 9; 14 t, 10.1 m, 4.4 m dia, 45 m³,
  4 crew, ~425 km, 51.6°, ~3-year life, ~2-week visits; Haven-2 (first module 2028 if selected; stretched Haven-1, later 7 m
  core). — https://aviationweek.com/space/space-exploration/vast-station-launch-slips-2027 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haven-1 ;
  https://www.vastspace.com/updates/vast-announces-haven-2-its-proposed-space-station-designed-to-succeed-the-international-space-station-iss
- Axiom: Dec 2024 re-plan → PPTM first, attaches to ISS, then undocks to join Hab-1 → free-flying 2-module station; PPTM
  slipped to ~2028 (Whitson, early 2026; single-source); Hab-1 4 crew; Thales Alenia builds primary structures. Ax-5 ordered
  30 Jan 2026, NET Jan 2027. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_Station ; https://behindtheblack.com/?p=120741 ;
  https://axiomspace.com/missions/ax5
- Starlab (Voyager/Airbus): CCDR Feb 2026; 8 m diameter, 450 m³, 4 crew, 60 kW, single Starship launch NET 2029. —
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starlab_(space_station)
- Orbital Reef (Blue Origin/Sierra Space): few public milestones since SDR; partnership reportedly strained → hedge.
- Russia ROS: Dec 2025 (Manturov) switch to 51.6° from ~97°; first module (UUM node, Baikonur) 2028 attached to Nauka, NEM 2029,
  airlock 2030, then separates from ISS. — https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/russia-scales-back-ambitions-for-its-next-space-station/ ;
  https://tass.com/science/2112209
- India BAS: BAS-01 approved Sept 2024 for 2028 (LVM-3, ~10 t); five modules by 2035; 1:1 model shown Aug 2025. —
  https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/india-plans-to-launch-bharatiya-antariksh-station-by-2028-enn25082206734
- Gateway: paused "in current form" 24 Mar 2026 (Moon Base announcement, ~$20 B over seven years, three phases); HALO stop-work
  April 2026 (contract $1.9 B, corrosion found by Thales Alenia); HALO hardware converted to three Lunar Infrastructure Demo
  (LID-1/2/3) surface missions (Aug 2026); PPE reportedly reused for SR-1 nuclear-electric Mars mission (~2028). —
  https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nasa-moon-base-plan-lunar-south-pole/ ;
  https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/06/nasa-northrop-grumman-repurpose-gateway-elements-for-moon-base-demo-missions/
