LC-39A Starship pad: first Florida Starship launch targeted late 2026 (not yet flown, Oct 2026). https://news.clearancejobs.com/2025/12/04/space-coast-shake-up-starship-launches-could-hit-76-per-year-at-slc-37/ ; https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2025/10/spacex-roberts-road-olm-preps/

- Dragon splashdowns moved to the Pacific off California from 2025 (Fram2 first), after trunk debris reached land (Australia 2022, North Carolina 2024); deorbit burn now before trunk jettison. https://www.space.com/spacex-crew-9-iss-astronaut-space-junk-dragon
- LC-39B (NASA reference page): IOP/SS water tower ~400 000 gal, released in < 30 s, peak flow 1.1 million gal/min; three ~600 ft lightning masts; new LH2 sphere ~1.25 million gal usable (≈1.4 million total, 83 ft diameter, glass-bubble insulation, boil-off −40–50 %); flame trench walls 96 000 bricks; deflector ~150 steel plates up to 4 000 lb; trench & deflector 3 000–5 600 °F; VAB 525 ft. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/launch-complex-39b/ ; https://www.brph.com/portfolio-items/liquid-hydrogen-lh2-system-at-launch-complex-39b/
- Shuttle sound suppression: 300 000 gal tower, started T−6.6 s, emptied in 41 s; Energiya pad 18 m³/s; Antares Pad 0A 250 000 gal tower, 4 000 gal/s. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_suppression_system
- Crawler-transporter (NASA fact sheet): CT-2 ≈ 6.65 million lb, capacity 18 million lb (upgraded from 12), 131 × 114 ft, 1 mph loaded / 2 mph unloaded, ~165 gal of diesel per mile, 4.2 miles to Pad 39B (~8 h trek); ML-1 ≈ 11.3 million lb; Artemis I vehicle ≈ 3.5 million lb. https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/combined_crawler-transporters_fact_sheet_final.pdf
- Mobile Launcher 2: NASA (Mar 2026) no longer plans to use EUS or ML-2 (IG: cost ~$1.8 B vs $383 M contract). https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/nasa-ig-slams-bechtel-on-mobile-launcher-2/ ; https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/mar/04/nasa-now-officially-has-no-plans-to-use-new-mobile/

Weather
- NASA TM-103498 (Mailander, 1990), Fig. I-22: mean thunderstorm days per month at CCAFS/Shuttle Landing Facility (1950–52, 1957–80): J 1, F 2, M 3, A 3, M 8, J 13, J 16, A 14, S 10, O 4, N 1, D 1 (≈ 76/yr; 80 % May–Sep). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19900018991/downloads/19900018991.pdf
- 45 WS (Maj. E. Graves, May 2020): 49 % of scrubs due to weather; 24 % of countdowns affected by weather. https://www.foxnews.com/us/weather-squadron-eye-on-sky-spacex-launch
- Gardner et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.07016): LLCC leading source of scrubs and delays; ~1/3 of countdowns delayed or scrubbed by lightning threats (Hazen et al. 1995); field-mill rule ±1 500 V/m; 12 rules from 1988 after AC-67 (1987); 140 of 159 successful launches 2005–2020 in hours with ≤ 15 % climatological field-mill violation probability. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07016
- Falcon 9 Crew Dragon launch weather criteria (NASA fact sheet FS-2020-05-568-KSC): 30 mph sustained wind at 162 ft; 30 min / 10 nmi lightning; anvils; 4 500 ft thick cloud layer; field mill ±1 500 V/m for 15 min; >50 downrange abort-weather locations. https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/falcon9_crewdragon_launch_weather_criteria_fact_sheet.pdf
- Falcon 9 countdown (NASA CRS-29 blog, approximate): T−38 min go for propellant load; T−35 RP-1 and stage-1 LOX loading; T−16 stage-2 LOX; T−7 engine chill; T−5 Dragon internal power; T−1 final checks/pressurisation; T−45 s go for launch; T−3 s ignition. https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/spacestation/2023/11/09/nasas-spacex-crs-29-countdown-and-ascent-milestones/
