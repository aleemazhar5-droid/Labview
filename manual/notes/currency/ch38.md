# Chapter 38 — Space Telescope Engineering: currency notes (checked 19 Oct 2026)

Facts after 2023, or numbers I was unsure of, with sources. Shared facts also taken from
`notes/currency_2026.md` (Roman launch) and `notes/currency/ch39.md` (Hubble orbit, PRIMA, Swift, XRISM gate valve,
Chandra budget, Gaia/Euclid/SPHEREx status) — not repeated here except where used with extra detail.

## Hubble
- Gyros: 8 of 22 gyros flown have failed (mostly corroded flex leads); SM4 (2009) replaced all six; one-gyro mode
  adopted 4 Jun 2024 after gyro 3 faults, science resumed mid-June 2024; one gyro in use + one in reserve. One-gyro
  mode uses magnetometers, sun sensors and star trackers in place of the other gyros, FGS for final pointing;
  efficiency −12 % (85→74 orbits/week); cannot track moving targets closer than Mars; reduced instantaneous sky
  access. NASA: >70 % probability of ≥1 working gyro through 2035.
  https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/design/hubble-one-gyro-mode/ ;
  https://www.space.com/hubble-telescope-one-gyroscope-mode ;
  https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/hubble-good-for-another-decade-despite-gyro-woes/
- STScI Hubble Users Committee (Fall 2025, newsletter 2026 vol. 43 issue 1): scheduling efficiency ~50–55 % in reduced
  gyro mode vs 55–58 % with three gyros; observing success rate back to >90 %; hardware listed as 3 FGS, 2 gyros,
  4 reaction wheels. https://www.stsci.edu/contents/newsletters/2026-volume-43-issue-01/fall-2025-meeting-of-hubble-space-telescope-users-committee-stuc
  (search-result summary; page returned 503 on direct fetch). No 2026 gyro failure or safe-mode reports found.
- Pointing stability requirement 0.007″ rms (7 mas) over 24 h; FGS; four reaction wheels.
  https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/design/pointing-control ; NTRS 19870013295
- Primary mirror 2.4 m, Corning ULE, ~828 kg (honeycomb core; ~3 600 kg solid equivalent), secondary 0.305 m,
  RC Cassegrain, Al + MgF₂ coating, mirrors held at ~21 °C. https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/design/optics/
- Mirror flaw: too flat at the edge (~2.2 µm), null-corrector spacing error. https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw
- Data: ~18 GB of science data per week (NASA "Hubble by the numbers" ~150 Gbit/week), relayed via TDRS.
  https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/about-hubble/hubble-by-the-numbers/

## JWST
- Ka-band 25.9 GHz science downlink up to 28 Mbit/s; ~57 GB/day (up to ~58.8 GB quoted); 68 GB solid-state
  recorder, ~3 % for engineering data; expected ~60 GB usable after 10 years.
  https://spectrum.ieee.org/james-webb-telescope-communications ; https://tomshardware.com/news/james-webb-space-telescope-uses-68gb-ssd
- Sunshield: 5 Kapton E layers (layer 1 50 µm, others 25 µm), ~100 nm Al coating, doped-Si coating on layers 1–2;
  ~21 m × 14 m; >200 kW of sunlight on layer 1, only ~23 mW reaches the telescope side; hot side ≈360–383 K
  (NASA model max 383 K), cold side ≈36–40 K; layers flared so heat escapes sideways.
  https://spectrum.ieee.org/james-webb-telescope-sunshield ; https://www.dupont.com/news/nasa-webb-telescope-keeping-cool-with-dupont-kapton.html
- MIRI: Si:As detectors must be < 7 K (nominal ~6.2 K, instrument ≤ 6.7 K); three-stage pulse-tube precooler (~¼ W
  at ~14 K) + Joule–Thomson loop; compressor in warm spacecraft bus ~10 m from MIRI; other instruments (HgCdTe)
  passively cooled to ~37–40 K. https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/cryocooler/
- Commissioning performance (Rigby et al. 2023 PASP, arXiv 2207.05632): guider precision ~1 mas (req. 4 mas), NIRCam
  jitter ~1 mas per axis, pointing stability ~6–7× better than requirement; observatory WFE at NIRCam 60–80 nm rms
  (prediction <100 nm; Cycle 1: corrections every 1–2 months keep it < 70 nm); segment stability < 10 nm rms between
  measurements every 2 days; propellant for > 20 years (req. 10.5 yr).
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05632 ; https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/cds/researchers/nac/apac/2022/Smith_JWST_APAC%20Oct2022_Tagged.pdf
- Micrometeoroid hit on segment C3, 22–24 May 2022: segment WFE ~56 → ~258–280 nm rms, corrected to 178 nm;
  adds ~9 nm rms to telescope total; five other strikes < 1 nm combined. Pointing restriction ("micrometeoroid
  avoidance zone", JWST user docs) limits time looking along the direction of orbital motion.
  https://www.astronomy.com/science/micrometeoroid-damage-to-james-webb-space-telescope-imaged-for-first-time/ ;
  https://jwst-docs.stsci.edu (Micrometeoroid Avoidance Zone page)
- Mirror: 18 hexagonal Be segments, ~1.32 m flat-to-flat, ~20 kg each, 7 actuators per segment (6 hexapod + 1 radius
  of curvature), gold coating ~100 nm (~50 g total). https://asme.org/topics-resources/content/designing-a-golden-eye-for-the-sky ;
  arXiv 2301.01779 (OTE paper)

## Roman
- Launched 30 Aug 2026 (Falcon Heavy, LC-39A); L2 insertion planned early Dec 2026; ~90-day commissioning; first
  science images early 2027. Coronagraph decontamination (warm detectors) during cruise (Sept 2026 reports).
  https://science.nasa.gov/missions/roman-space-telescope/roman-commissioning/ ; https://space.com/space-exploration/missions/roman-space-telescope-starts-waking-up-as-nasa-powers-on-instruments-for-the-1st-time
- Primary mirror 2.4 m, 186 kg (< ¼ Hubble's), reworked NRO-donated mirror; WFI 18 H4RG-10 (4k × 4k, 10 µm) ≈ 288
  Mpix ("300 megapixel"). https://photonics.com/Articles/Related_Primary_Mirror_Completed_for_NASAs/ar66166
- Data: up to ~11 Tbit (1 375 GB) compressed WFI data per day on average.
  https://roman-docs.stsci.edu/data-handbook-home/accessing-wfi-data

## Euclid
- 1.2 m SiC Korsch TMA; VIS 36 e2v CCDs (4096 × 4132, 12 µm) ≈ 609 Mpix; NISP 16 H2RG.
  https://www.euclid-ec.org/science/overview ; arXiv 2405.13491
- Water-ice contamination: throughput fell after launch (water absorbed by MLI on ground); decontamination
  12 Mar 2024 (fold + tertiary mirror heated to 160 K) and 6 Jun 2024 (fold mirror only); ~15 % throughput regained;
  stable since June 2024. arXiv 2503.15303 ; https://www.space.com/euclid-dark-matter-telescope-vision-restored

## X-ray / gamma / far-IR
- Chandra HRMA: 4 nested Wolter-I pairs (paraboloid + hyperboloid), Zerodur, Ir coating, outer diameters 1.23–0.65 m,
  focal length 10.07 m, HRMA mass 1 484 kg, PSF FWHM < 0.5″. https://cxc.cfa.harvard.edu/proposer/POG/html/HRMA.html
- XRISM Resolve: 6 × 6 (35 active) microcalorimeter at 50 mK (ADR); requirement ≤ 7 eV at 6 keV; ~4.5 eV FWHM
  achieved in orbit; gate valve still closed (ch39 notes). arXiv 2202.05399 ; arXiv 2506.06692
- Swift BAT: ~52 000 Pb tiles coded mask, 32 768 CZT detectors (4 × 4 × 2 mm), 5 200 cm², 1 m mask–detector gap.
  https://swift.gsfc.nasa.gov/about_swift/bat_desc.html
- Fermi LAT: pair-conversion (W foils + Si strip trackers + CsI calorimeter), ~20 MeV to >300 GeV. https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1046398
- Herschel: 3.5 m primary of 12 brazed SiC segments; blank machined 720 → 240 kg; telescope ~315–320 kg.
  https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Giant_Herschel_telescope_assembled
- PRIMA: selected 23 Sept 2026 as first Probe, 1.8 m (5.9 ft) far-IR telescope, $1.2 B cap, launch 2033 (ch39 notes,
  NASA release https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-selects-far-infrared-telescope-as-first-in-new-mission-class/).

## Starlight suppression / HWO
- Starshade technology closeout briefing to NASA, 14 Apr 2025 (no flight mission); lab starshade masks reached
  10⁻¹⁰ contrast over 12 % bandwidth at 2λ/D (Mennesson et al. 2024, arXiv 2404.18036); HWO baseline = coronagraph.
  https://science.nasa.gov/astrophysics/programs/exep/technology/starshade/
- HWO: Technology Maturation Project Office formed Aug 2024; aim TRL 5 before Mission Concept Review ~end of decade;
  needs ~10× Roman telescope stability, ~100× Roman CGI contrast, aperture ≥ 6 m; industry technology awards Jan 2026;
  FY2026 $150 M, FY2027 request $5 M (hedge). https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/habitable-worlds-observatory/hwo-meetings/aas-245-(jan-2025)/2_%20HWO%20TMPO.pdf

## Archives
- MAST: ~3 PB of file storage already in 2017 (20 missions); now "petabyte-scale", serving ~20 NASA missions incl.
  HST, JWST, TESS; Hubble public data on AWS since 2018. No current total found → write "several petabytes".
  https://archive.stsci.edu/archive_news/2017/10-Oct/index.html

## Additional checks (19 Oct 2026)
- Roman downlink: Ka band (26 GHz) up to 500 Mbit/s; ~1.4 TB of science data per day; stations NEN White Sands,
  ESA New Norcia, JAXA GREAT, DSN. https://science.nasa.gov/mission/roman-space-telescope/communications/ ;
  https://science.nasa.gov/mission/roman-space-telescope/observatory-technical/
- Euclid downlink: ~826–850 Gbit (~100 GB) compressed per day in a ~4 h daily pass to 35 m Cebreros or Malargüe,
  K band 26 GHz, up to ~75 Mbit/s. arXiv 2405.13491 ; https://esa.int/Enabling_Support/Operations/Euclid_calling_downloading_the_Universe
- Kepler: Earth-trailing heliocentric orbit, period 372.5 d, a = 1.013 AU, lag ~7°/yr; ~0.5 AU from Earth after 4
  years; retired 30 Oct 2018 after 9.5 years. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.1096 ; https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/the_kepler_mission_legacy.pdf
- HST zodiacal background (STIS handbook): high / average / low = 22.1 / 22.7 / 23.3 V mag/arcsec² (used 22.7 in
  the SNR model). https://etc.stsci.edu/etcstatic/users_guide/1_ref_9_background.html
