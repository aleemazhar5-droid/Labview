# Chapter 43 — AI Foundations: currency and fact notes (checked 25 Oct 2026)

History and early systems
- CLIPS (C Language Integrated Production System) developed from 1985 at NASA JSC (AI Section; origins 1984 in NASA text); written in C because LISP-based shells rarely reached operational use; now public domain. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLIPS ; Debian man page reproducing NASA history https://manpages.debian.org/jessie/clips/clips.1.en.html
- INCO Expert System Project began 1987 at JSC Mission Operations Directorate; INCO expert system (CLIPS-based) and a main-engine expert system first used in Mission Control on STS-26 (Sept–Oct 1988). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19900045447 ; https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19960001823
- SPIKE (STScI) long-range scheduler for Hubble, AI/constraint-based ("suitability functions"), in operational use since shortly after HST launch (April 1990); included a stochastic neural-network (GDS network) scheduler (Johnston & Adorf, Computers & OR 19, 1992); inspired the min-conflicts heuristic (Minton et al.). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19910013462 ; https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930009471
- SKICAT (JPL/Caltech, Fayyad, Weir, Djorgovski): decision trees (GID3*, O-BTree) for star/galaxy classification in POSS-II; ~94 % accuracy on faint objects (ICML 1993), ~90 % or better about 1 mag above plate limit (later papers); tripled number of classified objects. https://mlanthology.org/icml/1993/fayyad1993icml-skicat ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/9612108
- Deep Space 1 Remote Agent, May 1999 (mention only; Chapter 48).
- EO-1 Autonomous Sciencecraft Experiment: onboard classifiers (manual and linear SVM) on Mongoose V payload processor (8 MIPS, 256 MB RAM); 12 Hyperion bands accessible onboard, 11 used; five classes (land, water, snow, ice, cloud); linear SVM = 11 multiplies + 11 adds per class; trained on 100 expert-labelled images (50 train/50 test, ~91 M test pixels); overall accuracy SVM 81.3 % (83.8 % if unclassified assigned to water), expert 82.9 %, decision tree (C4.5) 80.9 %; ice = 2 % of test pixels, SVM ice recall 47.8 %; running onboard >1 yr (2006 paper), SVM used onboard "for over a dozen years"; Mt Erebus detection 7 May 2004; ~100× science return per MB claimed. Castano et al. KDD 2006 https://ai.jpl.nasa.gov/public/papers/castano-kdd06-classifiers.pdf ; https://esto.nasa.gov/forums/estf2019/presentations/Posters/Chien_ASE_SWB_estf_final_S.pdf
- MEXAR2 (ESA/ISTC-CNR) in daily use for Mars Express downlink planning since Feb 2005; ESA: workload cut ~50 %, data loss largely eliminated. https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Mars_Express/Artificial_intelligence_boosts_science_from_Mars
- AEGIS on Curiosity in routine use since May 2016 (ChemCam targeting); >93 % selection of desired target vs ~24 % without (Francis et al. 2017); used on Opportunity from 2010. https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/laser-targeting-ai-yields-more-mars-science
- Kalman filter (Kalman 1960); Stanley Schmidt at NASA Ames adapted it (extended/Schmidt–Kalman) for Apollo circumlunar navigation in early 1960s; flew in Apollo guidance. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_F._Schmidt ; https://www.nasa.gov/feature/ames/math-invented-for-moon-landing-helps-your-flight-arrive-on-time

Deep-learning era milestones
- AlexNet (2012) ImageNet top-5 error 15.3 % vs 26.2 % runner-up (well established, pre-2024).
- Transformer (Vaswani et al., June 2017); "foundation model" term (Bommasani et al., Stanford, Aug 2021).
- Telemanom (Hundman et al., KDD 2018, JPL): LSTM + nonparametric thresholds on SMAP (55 channels) and MSL (27 channels), 105 labelled anomaly sequences; precision 87.5 %, recall 80.0 %; piloted by SMAP ops on >700 channels since Oct 2017, not central due to false positives. https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1802.04431
- Deep Mars (Wagstaff et al., AAAI 2018): AlexNet fine-tuned (transfer learning) to classify HiRISE (3 820 crops, 168 images) and MSL images for the PDS Imaging Atlas; deployed. https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11404
- ESA Kelvins Spacecraft Collision Avoidance Challenge (16 Oct–16 Dec 2019): 15 321 events, 199 082 CDMs (2015–19), 103 attributes; 515 events with final risk ≥10⁻⁶, 131 ≥10⁻⁵, 30 ≥10⁻⁴; 97 teams, 862 submissions; naive latest-risk baseline L = 0.694, only 12 of 97 teams beat it. Uriot et al., Astrodynamics 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03069 ; https://kelvins.esa.int/collision-avoidance-challenge/results
- Φsat-1 (ESA, cubesat in FSSCat) launched 2/3 Sept 2020 on Vega VV16; onboard cloud-detection CNN on Intel Movidius Myriad 2; first ESA onboard deep learning for EO. https://www.satellitetoday.com/technology/2020/10/22/intel-provides-smarts-for-first-satellite-with-local-ai-processing-on-board
- OPS-SAT (ESA) 18 Dec 2019 – 22 May 2024: in-orbit neural-network deployment, onboard training and retraining (2021–22 papers). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPS-SAT
- Φsat-2 (ESA/Open Cosmos, 6U) launched 16 Aug 2024 on Transporter-11 with Ubotica CogniSAT AI apps. https://esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Phsat-2/New_satellite_demonstrates_the_power_of_AI_for_Earth_observation
- Prithvi (NASA–IBM) geospatial foundation model 2023; Prithvi-EO-2.0 (Dec 2024): 300 M and 600 M parameters, ViT masked autoencoder, ~4.2 M HLS samples. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02732
- ECMWF AIFS Single operational 25 Feb 2025 (shared fact sheet).
- JPL Dynamic Targeting on CogniSAT-6 (Ubotica/Open Cosmos, launched Mar 2024 Transporter-10): flights Jul–Aug 2025; look-ahead cloud avoidance; 45–50° look-ahead ≈74–90 s before nadir at 500 km. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05304 ; https://ai.jpl.nasa.gov/public/documents/papers/DT-Astra-2025-Extended-Abstract.pdf
- Starcloud-1 (≈60 kg, H100 GPU) launched 2 Nov 2025; trained a small language model in orbit (Dec 2025). Single demo satellite. https://oodaloop.com/briefs/technology/greetings-earthlings-nvidia-backed-starcloud-trains-first-ai-model-in-space-as-orbital-data-center-race-heats-up/
- Perseverance: first drives with waypoints generated by a generative vision-language model, 8 Dec 2025 (210 m) and 10 Dec 2025 (246 m); validated on digital twin (>500 000 telemetry variables). https://www.therobotreport.com/nasa-perseverance-rover-completes-first-ai-planned-drive/  (model/vendor not named in text per style rule)
- Planet Pelican-4: onboard aircraft detection over Alice Springs, 25 Mar 2026, NVIDIA Jetson Orin; ~80 % detection accuracy on raw imagery (company figure). https://investors.planet.com/news/news-details/2026/Planet-Successfully-Runs-AI-in-Space/
- Google/Planet Project Suncatcher prototype (4 TPUs) launched 1 Oct 2026 on Transporter-18; one-year test. https://www.gpb.org/news/2026/10/01/google-launches-project-suncatcher-step-towards-ai-data-centers-in-space ; https://www.space.com/space-exploration/satellites/spacex-google-project-suncatcher-ai-satellite-transporter-18-mission

Classical ML in operational space products
- MODIS land cover MCD12Q1: C5 used C4.5 decision tree; C6/C6.1 uses Random Forest on annual spectro-temporal metrics + HMM post-processing; 500 m, annual 2001–present. MCD12 User Guide V6.1 https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1409/MCD12_User_Guide_V61.pdf
- s2cloudless (Sentinel Hub): LightGBM gradient-boosting, 10 Sentinel-2 bands; basis of GEE COPERNICUS/S2_CLOUD_PROBABILITY. https://pypi.org/project/s2cloudless/ ; https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_S2_CLOUD_PROBABILITY
- Hansen et al. 2013 Global Forest Change: ~654 000 Landsat scenes, 143 billion pixels / 20 terapixels, 1 M CPU-core hours on 10 000 computers (Google Earth Engine), decision-tree classifiers. https://research.google/blog/the-first-detailed-maps-of-global-forest-change/
- Finnish Multi-Source NFI: k-NN estimation with satellite images + field plots, developed from 1989 (Tomppo). https://www.isprs.org/proceedings/XXIX/congress/part7/671_XXIX-part7.pdf
- MCSST (McClain, Pichel & Walton 1985): coefficients by regression against drifting buoys; rms ~0.5 °C (NOAA-9). https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/pub/sod/osb/aignatov/SST_Alg_Refs/McClainEtAl_1985_JGR_Comparative_Performance_JC090iC06p11587.pdf
- IASI 8 461 channels; EUMETSAT disseminates PC scores since Feb 2011 (EumetCast); reconstruction with ~300 PCs (JEDI docs). https://nwp-saf.eumetsat.int/publications/vs_reports/nwpsaf-mf-vs-005.pdf ; https://jcsda-jedi-docs.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/latest/inside/jedi-components/ufo/variabletransforms/transforms/radfrompcscores.html
- Bobra & Couvidat 2015 (ApJ 798:135): RBF-kernel SVM on SDO/HMI SHARP features (25 → 13), 303 flaring (≥M1) vs 5 000 non-flaring; TSS 0.761 ± 0.039 (24 h), 0.817 ± 0.034 (48 h). https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1411.1405
- Haywood et al. 2014 (MNRAS 443): Gaussian-process model of stellar activity in CoRoT-7 RVs; masses 4.73 ± 0.95 and 13.56 ± 1.08 M⊕. https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.1044
- Ploton et al. 2020 (Nat. Commun. 11:4540): random forest biomass map, central Africa, 11.8 M trees; random 10-fold CV R² = 0.53 vs spatial CV R² = 0.14; buffered LOO ~0 beyond 100 km; AGB autocorrelated to ~120 km. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7486894/
- Galaxy Zoo (Jul 2007): ~50 M classifications by ~150 000 volunteers in first year. Walmsley et al. 2020 (MNRAS 491): Bayesian CNN + active learning needs 35–60 % fewer labelled galaxies. https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07424
- BigEarthNet 590 326 Sentinel-2 patches (2019); EuroSAT 27 000 images, 10 classes (2019). https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06148

Data volumes
- NASA EOSDIS archive 178.7 PB (FY2025, 116.2 PB in cloud), growth ~160 TB/day; passed 100 PB Oct 2023. https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/about/data-metrics

Fact-check additions (independent check, 25 Oct 2026)
- Kelvins collision-avoidance challenge: paper abstract/Sec. 5 say 96 teams, 862 submissions (elsewhere in the same paper "97 teams"); 12 teams beat the latest-risk baseline L = 0.694. Chapter uses 96. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.03069

- EO-1 classifiers (Castano et al.
