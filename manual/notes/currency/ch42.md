## Nuclear electric propulsion and fission power
- SR-1 Freedom (Space Reactor-1 Freedom): announced by Isaacman ~24 Mar 2026 together with SkyFall (three
  Ingenuity-class Mars helicopters); NASA + DOE; NEP using the Gateway Power and Propulsion Element (PPE) bus;
  reactor ≥ 20 kWe, HALEU UO2 fuel, heat pipes, boron-carbide shield, closed Brayton conversion; EP system "up to
  48 kW" (NASA diagram); reactor switched on only after leaving Earth orbit (~2 days after launch); launch
  late/December 2028; Mars ~1 yr later. Cost ≈ $2.1 B without helicopters (Ars Technica, 1 Sep 2026, via Wikipedia).
  Oct 20 2026: Sierra Lobo to supply closed-Brayton power-conversion system (NASA Glenn); reactor designed by INL.
  DOE conditionally committed HALEU (July 2026). Space Reactor Office created May 2026.
  https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/nasas-nuclear-mission-to-mars-isnt-as-crazy-as-it-sounds/ (31 Mar 2026)
  https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/04/14/1135848/nasa-nuclear-powered-spacecraft
  https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/glenn/sr-1-freedom-advances-with-progress-on-nuclear-power-module (20 Oct 2026)
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Reactor%E2%80%911_Freedom (rev. 16 Oct 2026; thrusters: 3 × 12 kW AEPS + 4 × 6 kW Busek)
- PPE builder: Lanteris Space Systems (ex-Maxar Space Systems), acquired by Intuitive Machines (closed 13 Jan 2026).
  https://www.satellitetoday.com/manufacturing/2026/01/13/intuitive-machines-completes-lanteris-space-systems-acquisition/
- NSTM-3 (OSTP, 14 Apr 2026) "National Initiative for American Space Nuclear Power": reactors in orbit as early as
  2028, on the Moon by 2030; SR-1 Dec 2028; Lunar Reactor-1 (LR-1) to land 2030; DoW mid-power in-space reactor by 2031.
  https://spaceq.ca/white-house-directs-accelerated-space-nuclear-power-initiative/
- Duffy directive (31 Jul / 5 Aug 2025): lunar reactor ≥ 100 kWe, launch-ready ~2030 (vs earlier 40 kWe FSP).
  https://www.upi.com/Science_News/2025/08/05/science-sean-duffy-nuclear-reactor-moon/2301754399672/
- LR-1: draft SPARC IDIQ (NASA Glenn, 30 Aug 2026) specifies a flight system of ≥ 20 kWe; Phase 1 up to 18 months
  to PDR; awards not yet made (as of 1 Sep 2026). SR-1 is described as "precursor to LR-1".
  https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/09/01/what-does-nasas-sparc-lunar-reactor-procurement-reveal-about-lunar-reactor-1/
- KRUSTY (Kilopower): Nov 2017–Mar 2018 at Nevada National Security Site; 28-h full-power run; HEU U-235 core;
  sodium heat pipes → Stirling convertors; Kilopower family 1–10 kWe.
  https://www.y12.doe.gov/node/1066 ; https://www.rdworldonline.com/nasa-successfully-tests-its-nuclear-fission-power-system/
- FSP Technology Demonstration Unit photo (GRC-2015-C-06315): 10 kW Stirling PCU; flight concept 180 kWt → 40 kWe.

## Nuclear thermal propulsion
- DRACO (DARPA/NASA, HALEU NTP flight demo, Lockheed Martin/BWXT): ended 2025 — NASA FY2026 PBR (30 May 2025)
  zeroed NTP/NEP; DARPA confirmed closeout (June 2025); reasons: cost, cheaper launch, ground-test/approval difficulty.
  https://breakingdefense.com/2025/06/darpas-draco-nuclear-propulsion-project-roars-no-more/
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstration_Rocket_for_Agile_Cislunar_Operations
- FY2026 CJS appropriation: "no less than $110 million" for NTP (Sen. Britt, Apr 2026 hearing; Isaacman agreed to
  implement). https://www.britt.senate.gov/?p=12220
- Rover/NERVA (1955–Jan 1973): Kiwi (8 reactors, 1959–64), Phoebus-2A 4 082 MW (26 Jun 1968), Pewee 503 MW,
  2 550 K exit gas (Dec 1968), NF-1 44 MW 1972; ~$1.4 B. NERVA XE-Prime 1969: ~1 140 MW, 246.7 kN, 28 starts
  (some sources 24), Isp 841 s vac. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Rover ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NERVA
  (Pewee Isp quoted as 845–901 s in different sources → text says "about 850–900 s").
- Russia Zeus / TEM: megawatt-class NEP tug, Keldysh/Arsenal; 2030 target reported 2020–21; no 2025–26 confirmation
  found → hedged. https://www.russianspaceweb.com/tem.html
- China: reported ground tests of a megawatt-class space reactor concept (2024 paper, secondary source) → hedged.

## Electric propulsion
- AEPS (12 kW magnetically shielded Hall, L3Harris/Aerojet + NASA GRC): three flight thrusters accepted and delivered
  2025 (third Sept 2025, to Lanteris); environmental qualification complete; life demonstration being prepared (NASA
  SP2026 paper, NTRS 20260002899). ~2 600 s at 600 V, ~0.6 N.
  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260002899 ; https://images.nasa.gov/details/grc-2025-c-07151
- NEXT-C on DART: 6.9 kW, ~4 190 s, ~236 mN max; first activation 18 Dec 2021; power-electronics anomaly (current
  step) during recycle → NESC investigation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEXT_(ion_thruster)
- VASIMR VX-200SS: 88 h at 80 kW (12–16 Jul 2021), short of 100 h goal; no newer test found.
  https://satnews.com/2021/07/22/ad-astras-vasimr-plasma-rocket-champions-high-power-endurance-test/
- Enpulsion indium FEEP: > 200 systems launched (IEPC 2024 paper). https://www.enpulsion.com/wp-content/uploads/IEPC-2024-279.pdf

## Solar sails
- ACS3: launched 23/24 Apr 2024 (Electron, Mahia); 12U, 16 kg; ~1 000 km SSO; 80 m² sail on four 7 m composite
  booms; deployed 28/29 Aug 2024 (primary objective met); one boom bent, not actively controlled (Oct 2024 reports);
  NASA "Mission Results and Lessons Learned" SmallSat 2026 — challenges in all phases.
  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260004973 ; https://www.nasa.gov/smallspacecraft/what-is-acs3 ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Composite_Solar_Sail_System ; https://space.skyrocket.de/doc_sdat/acs3.htm
- LightSail 2: 32 m², ~720 km; raised apogee ~2 km in first days (Jul 2019); re-entered 17 Nov 2022 after ~3.5 yr.
  https://www.planetary.org/articles/lightsail-2-completes-mission
- NEA Scout: 86 m², ~14 kg 6U; Artemis I (16 Nov 2022), never contacted, lost.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-Earth_Asteroid_Scout
- Solar Cruiser: ~1 670 m² sail; not confirmed for Phase C; closeout 2023. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Cruiser
- IKAROS: 14 m × 14 m (196 m²), 310–315 kg, measured SRP 1.12 mN (JAXA, 9 Jul 2010); Venus flyby 8 Dec 2010.
  https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2010/07/20100709_ikaros_e.html

## Beamed and interstellar
- Breakthrough Starshot: up to 100 GW phased laser array (~1 km², ~3 km combining optics for 4 m sail), gram-scale
  sail+chip, ~10 000 g for ~10 min, 15–20 % c, 20–30 yr to α Cen; Parkin 2018 cost-optimum: 4.1 m sail, 9 min,
  ~$8 B beam director. Programme paused 2024 (Benford, Centauri Dreams 3 Mar 2026; SciAm Sept 2025 "on hold
  indefinitely"; ~$4.5 M of $100 M spent per Lubin).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Starshot ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01306 ;
  https://www.centauri-dreams.org/2026/03/03/starshot-is-a-success-part-i/
- Direct Fusion Drive (PSS/PPPL PFRC): concept 2.5–5 N per MW, ~10 000 s; PFRC-2 lab device; no 2025–26 milestone
  found → "laboratory stage". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_field-reversed_configuration
- Antimatter: CERN lifetime production < 10 ng; ~10¹² antiprotons/day, ~200 days/yr.
  https://cerncourier.com/a/setting-the-record-straight/

## Air-breathing / detonation
- JAXA/Nagoya S-520-31 (27 Jul 2021): RDE 500 N for 6 s in space + PDE 3 × 2 s.
  https://www.nagoya-u.ac.jp/researchinfo/result-en/2021/09/20210903-001.html
- NASA MSFC RDRE: 2022 > 4 000 lbf ~1 min at 622 psia; 27 Sep 2023 251-s hot fire > 5 800 lbf.
  https://images.nasa.gov/details/09-27-2023-HF-12%20_062
- Venus Aerospace: first US flight test of an RDRE, Spaceport America, announced 14 May 2025 (limited data).
  https://www.militaryaerospace.com/commercial-aerospace/article/55290679/
- Reaction Engines: administration 31 Oct 2024 (PwC); IP sale to preferred bidder near completion June 2025 (buyer
  not named). https://www.aerospacetestinginternational.com/news/reaction-engines-enters-administration.html

## Propellantless
- TSS-1R (STS-75, 25 Feb 1996): 19.7 km of 20.7 km deployed when tether broke (arcing); ~3 500 V, ~0.48 A.
  https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/space/text/tss-1r_results.txt
- EmDrive: TU Dresden (Tajmar et al., 2021) reproduced "thrust" as thermal/mounting artefact; anomalous thrust bounded
  below classical photon-rocket level (≥ 3 orders below earlier claims); 2018 study: cable/magnetic-field artefact.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EmDrive
