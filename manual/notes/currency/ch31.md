# Chapter 31 — Crewed Spacecraft: currency notes (post-2023 facts; checked 18 Oct 2026)

## Boeing Starliner
- Crew Flight Test launched 5 Jun 2024 (Atlas V N22); RCS thruster failures on approach (doghouse heating, Teflon poppet seals
  deformed), helium leaks (7 of 8 manifolds); Starliner returned uncrewed to White Sands 7 Sep 2024 (UTC); Wilmore and
  Williams returned on Crew-9 Dragon in March 2025 (18 Mar 2025). Feb 2026 NASA investigation reclassified CFT as a Type A
  mishap. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Starliner (checked 18 Oct 2026)
- Nov 2025 contract change: Starliner-1 converted to uncrewed cargo flight (then NET Apr 2026). —
  https://spaceflightnow.com/2025/11/24/nasa-boeing-pivot-starliner-1-mission-from-4-person-astronaut-flight-to-cargo-only/
- 28 Sep 2026 Boeing/NASA update: Starliner-1 (uncrewed cargo) window Dec 2026–Jan 2027; crewed Starliner-2 planned 2028;
  missions 5 and 6 restored; fixes: new valves on all 12 crew-module RCS thrusters, redesigned SM thruster poppets, parachute
  joint improvement, NASA-provided batteries; 19 of 21 CFT in-flight anomalies closed; Vulcan to be certified for crew. —
  https://www.boeing.com/features/2026/09/nasa-boeing-update-starliner-contract-and-set-launch-window-for-next-mission
- Starliner specs: up to 7 crew (NASA flies 4), 11 m³ pressurised, 4.56 m diameter, 4 RS-88 abort engines (40 000 lbf each),
  20 OMAC 1 500 lbf thrusters, three mains + six airbags, land landing, reusable up to 10 times. Pad abort test 4 Nov 2019
  (White Sands; 160 000 lbf combined; one main chute did not deploy). — Wikipedia Boeing Starliner; NASA image KSC-20191104-PH-JSC01_0002

## Orion / Artemis II
- Artemis II launched 1 Apr 2026 22:35 UTC from LC-39B; crew Wiseman, Glover, Koch, Hansen (CSA); Orion CM-003 "Integrity";
  max distance 406 771 km (252 756 mi), beating Apollo 13; closest approach ≈ 6 545 km from far side on 6 Apr; splashdown
  10 Apr 2026 17:07 PDT (11 Apr 00:07 UTC) off San Diego; 9 d 1 h 32 min; recovery USS John P. Murtha. Anomalies: frozen
  urine vent line (FD3), small ESM helium leak (FD8) cancelled a manual-control test; comms issue after splashdown. Entry ≈ 11 km/s
  (24 664 mph), did not beat Apollo 10's speed record. Manual piloting demonstration completed. —
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_II (checked 18 Oct 2026)
- NASA post-flight heat-shield assessment (15 Oct 2026): 9 char-loss spots vs 103 on Artemis I (≈ tenfold reduction), credited to a
  shorter maximum downrange distance ("lofted"/modified entry instead of full skip); entry within 192 ft of target entry interface;
  splashdown 0.59 mi from target; peak surface > 4 000 °F; 186 Avcoat blocks; 1 300 backshell tiles; Artemis III shield has
  uniform-permeability Avcoat, installed July 2026; Artemis III in LEO "next year" (2027). —
  https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/artemis-2/rigorous-analysis-confirms-nasas-artemis-ii-heat-shield-performance/
- Orion specs: CM 10.4 t launch / 9.3 t dry; CM+ESM 26.5 t; with LAS 33.4 t; pressurised 19.56 m³, habitable 8.9 m³; 5.0 m
  diameter; 11 kW arrays; 21 days. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_(spacecraft)
- Orion parachutes: 11 total (3 forward-bay-cover, 2 drogues 23 ft/7 m, 3 pilots, 3 mains 116 ft/35 m), from ~350 mph to < 20 mph.
  — NASA CPAS articles (search 18 Oct 2026)
- Orion LAS: abort motor ≈ 400 000 lbf (AA-2 reports; PA-1 design figure 500 000 lbf); ACM > 7 000 lbf; jettison motor > 40 000 lbf.
  AA-2 2 Jul 2019: abort at ≈ 31 000 ft (9.5 km), ≈ Mach 1.1, ~50 s after launch. PA-1 6 May 2010: 445 mph in 3 s, ≈ 1.9 km apogee.
  — https://spaceflightnow.com/2019/07/02/nasa-successfully-tests-orion-launch-abort-system-before-moon-flights/ ; NASA PA-1 releases

## Artemis III / landers
- Artemis III restructured (2026) as a crewed Earth-orbit test with landers; Isaacman (Apr 2026): NET late 2027; first landing
  Artemis IV ~2028. Starship reached orbit Flight 14 (28 Sep 2026); ship-to-ship propellant-transfer demo not yet flown (Sep 2026).
  — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_HLS ; https://spaceq.ca/blue-origin-lunar-lander-cabin-mock-up-arrives-as-nasas-artemis-face-delays/
- Blue Moon MK2: 16 m, > 45 t, three BE-7 LOX/LH2, SLD contract May 2023 ≈ US$3.4 B; MK2 prototype without full propulsion may fly to
  Earth orbit for Artemis III docking test; cabin mock-up delivered to JSC. MK1 pathfinder 2027. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_(spacecraft)

## Crew Dragon
- Crew-11 returned early (first ISS medical evacuation): undocked 14 Jan 2026, splashdown off San Diego 15 Jan 2026 (Endeavour). —
  https://www.npr.org/2026/01/15/nx-s1-5677647/nasa-spacex-crew-11-splashdown-return
- Crew-13 launched 1 Oct 2026 11:10 EDT on Dragon "Grace" (2nd flight; first was Ax-4, Jun 2025); docked in 7 h 55 min (fastest
  Dragon); Watkins, Delaney, Kutryk (CSA), Teteryatnikov; NASA's 13th operational Dragon mission. Crew-12 (Freedom) Pacific
  splashdown planned ~8 Oct 2026. — https://spacepolicyonline.com/?p=91107
- Fram2 (31 Mar/1 Apr 2025) first crewed polar orbit (Resilience); first of the West-Coast-return era; trunk now jettisoned after
  deorbit burn so it falls in ocean (change announced Jul 2024). — https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2025/03/fram2-launch/ ;
  https://www.space.com/spacex-crew-9-iss-astronaut-space-junk-dragon
- Specs: 9.3 m³ pressurised; launch mass ≈ 12.5 t; 8 SuperDraco ≈ 71 kN each; IFA 19 Jan 2020 abort ≈ 84 s, peak 3.3 g,
  ≈ 130 000 lbf total. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2 ; https://spaceflightnow.com/2020/01/23/spacex-releases-preliminary-results-from-crew-dragon-abort-test/

## Soyuz
- Soyuz MS-28 launched 27 Nov 2025 (Kud-Sverchkov, Mikayev, C. Williams); Site 31 service cabin collapsed into flame trench; repairs
  complete 3 Mar 2026; Progress MS-33 launched 22 Mar 2026. — https://spacepolicyonline.com/news/russia-completes-repairs-to-site-31-next-cargo-launch-on-march-22/
- Soyuz MS-29 launched 14 Jul 2026 (Dubrov, Kikina, Menon), landing planned ~Apr 2027. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_MS-29
- Soyuz MS specs: 7 080 kg; orbital module 5.0 m³, descent module 3.5 m³ (2 950 kg); soft-landing engines ~1 m above ground.
  — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_(spacecraft)
- Historical (verified): Soyuz T-10-1 26 Sep 1983 (Titov, Strekalov), tower fired ~6 s before explosion, 14–17 g for 5 s, landed 4 km
  away; Soyuz MS-10 11 Oct 2018 (Ovchinin, Hague), booster separation collision (deformed ball-joint/sensor pin), abort ~50 km,
  apogee 93 km, 6–7 g ballistic, landed 19 min 41 s after launch ~400 km downrange. — Wikipedia Soyuz T-10-1, Soyuz MS-10

## Safety data
