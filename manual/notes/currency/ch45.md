# Chapter 45 — Data, Computing Hardware and Edge AI in Orbit: currency and fact notes (checked 25 Oct 2026)

Data volumes
- NASA EOSDIS archive 178.7 PB at end FY2025 (116.2 PB in cloud), growth ~160 TB/day (reused from ch43 notes). https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/about/data-metrics
- Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem annual report (released 24 Sep 2026, 2025 data): ~91 PB online data end-2025, >120 million Sentinel products, 542 622 registered users (Dec 2025), 129.9 PB direct Sentinel downloads in 14.7 billion events, >242 PB delivered through all channels. https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/09/28/can-europe-turn-copernicus-data-infrastructure-into-a-commercial-growth-platform/
- CDSE opened January 2023; Copernicus Open Access Hub (SciHub) ceased operations 2 Nov 2023. https://www.sinergise.com/en/node/363 ; https://scihub.copernicus.eu/news/News01233
- NISAR: NASA Earthdata says close to 85 TB of data products per day, up to ~140 PB over the 3-year mission (an AWS case study gives ~70 TB/day). https://earthdata.nasa.gov/news/feature-articles/getting-ready-nisar-managing-big-data-using-commercial-cloud
- NISAR downlink: requirement ≥26 Tbit/day radar science data; Ka-band 4.0 Gbps to NASA stations (two transmitters, dual polarisation, 2 Gsps each) and 2.88 Gbps to ISRO. https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/press-kits/nisar/quick-facts ; https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210008861
- Rubin Observatory: ~20 TB per night, ~10 million alerts per night with ~60 s latency, ~60 PB raw data over 10 years; LSST officially began 30 Jun 2026 after commissioning (operations handover Oct 2025); 3 200-megapixel camera, image every ~40 s. https://rubinobservatory.org/explore/how-rubin-works/numbers ; https://www.astronomy.com/science/vera-c-rubin-observatory-begins-its-legacy-survey-of-space-and-time/ ; https://www.washington.edu/news/2026/06/30/rubin-observatory-legacy-survey-space-time-lsst/
- Google Earth Engine: catalogue described as >90 PB analysis-ready data and >1 000 datasets (Cloud page) / "over eighty petabytes" (main page); free for academic/research, available for commercial use. https://cloud.google.com/earth-engine ; https://earthengine.google.com/
- Microsoft Planetary Computer: JupyterHub compute retired 6 Jun 2024; data catalogue and STAC API continue; Planetary Computer Pro is a paid Azure GeoCatalog service for private data. https://github.com/microsoft/PlanetaryComputer/discussions/347 ; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/planetary-computer
- Cloud cover: MODIS (King et al. 2013, IEEE TGRS 51(7)) global cloud fraction ~67 %, ~55 % over land, ~72 % over ocean. https://modis-atmosphere.gsfc.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/ModAtmo/King_et_al.2013.pdf

Benchmarks and datasets
- EuroSAT 27 000 images, 10 classes (2019); BigEarthNet 590 326 Sentinel-2 patches (2019) (ch43 notes). reBEN / BigEarthNet v2.0 (Clasen et al., arXiv 2407.03653, 2024–25): 549 488 Sentinel-1/2 patch pairs, 1 200 m × 1 200 m, pixel-level reference maps from CORINE 2018, geographic splits. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03653
- SEN12MS (Schmitt et al. 2019): 180 662 triplets of Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 multispectral and MODIS land cover patches. https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07789
- So2Sat LCZ42 (Zhu et al. 2020, IEEE GRSM): 400 673 Sentinel-1/2 patch pairs (32 × 32 px, 320 m) in 42 urban agglomerations + 10 smaller areas; 17 LCZ classes; labelled by 15 domain experts over six months; overall label confidence 85 %. https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12171
- xView (2018): >1 million objects, 60 classes, >1 400 km² of WorldView-3 imagery at 0.3 m. https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07856
- fMoW (Christie et al., CVPR 2018): 1 047 691 images, 63 categories (incl. false detection), 207 of 247 ISO country codes; ~3.5 TB multispectral / ~200 GB RGB. https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07846 ; https://github.com/fMoW/dataset
- xBD (2019): 850 736 building annotations over 45 362 km², 19 disasters, 22 068 images of 1 024 × 1 024 px. https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09296
- CloudSEN12 (Aybar et al., Sci. Data Dec 2022): 49 400 image patches (paper body 49 250: 10 000 high-quality, 10 000 scribble, 29 250 unlabelled), 5 090 m × 5 090 m, Sentinel-2 L1C/L2A + Sentinel-1; CC-BY-NC 4.0 on HF/GEE. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9789947/
- SpaceNet 8 (2022): 850 km², 32 000 buildings, 1 300 km roads, flood labels; SpaceNet series hosted on AWS Open Data (Maxar imagery). https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/EarthVision/papers/Hansch_SpaceNet_8_-_The_Detection_of_Flooded_Roads_and_Buildings_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf
- SPEED+ (Park et al. 2021, SPEC2021 with ESA ACT): 59 960 synthetic images (OpenGL renderer, 80:20 train/val) + 9 531 hardware-in-the-loop images (6 740 lightbox, 2 791 sunlamp) from Stanford TRON facility; Tango spacecraft (PRISMA). https://purl.stanford.edu/wv398fc4383 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03101
- Telemanom SMAP/MSL (Hundman et al. 2018): 55 + 27 channels, 105 labelled anomaly sequences (ch43 notes).
- ESA-ADB (Kotowski et al., arXiv 2406.17826, Jun 2024, v2 Aug 2025): ESA Anomalies Dataset from 3 missions, 2 in benchmark. Mission1: 76 channels (58 target), 14 years (anonymised), 774.9 M points; Mission2: 100 channels (47 target), 3.5 years, 776.7 M points; total 1.55 billion points, 7.32 GB compressed, 844 annotated events (148 anomalies, 690 rare nominal events). Zenodo doi 10.5281/zenodo.12528696. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17826
- ESA Kelvins closed competitions: Mars Express Power Challenge (2016), PROBA-V super-resolution (Nov 2018–Jun 2019), Pose Estimation (SPEC 2019), Collision Avoidance (2019), Pose Estimation 2021 (SPEED+), spotGEO, Star Trackers: First Contact, The Kessler Run, Space Debris: The Origin, Planetary Defence, OPS-SAT case (Jul–Oct 2022, quantisation-aware few-shot model to run on OPS-SAT), ELOPE. https://kelvins.esa.int/ ; https://kelvins.esa.int/opssat/challenge/
- NASA Frontier Development Lab: created 2016 by NASA, SETI Institute and Trillium Technologies (Trillium co-managed to 2024); 8–10-week research sprints; FDL Europe with ESA (Oxford). https://seti.org/fdl

Edge AI missions and onboard processing
- Φsat-1: launched 2 (UTC 3) Sep 2020, Vega VV16, ~530 km SSO; Myriad 2 VPU, 36 h CERN beam test late 2018 + two more tests, passed unmodified; discarding cloudy images saves ~30 % bandwidth; trained on synthetic data from existing missions; ESA cites ~two-thirds of Earth cloud-covered. https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/2020/10/intel-powers-first-satellite-with-ai-on-board/
- Φsat-2: 6U, launched 16 Aug 2024 Transporter-11; apps on NanoSat MO Framework (installable/updatable from ground): Sat2Map (CGI), Cloud Detection (KP Labs), Vessel Detection (CEiiA), Deep Image Compression (GEO-K) at launch; Marine anomalies (IRT Saint Exupéry) and PhiFire AI (Thales Alenia Space) uploaded after launch; operational phase Apr 2025–Mar 2026 fine-tuning apps. https://open-cosmos.com/news/phisat-2-launch ; https://ceos.org/document_management/Working_Groups/WGISS/Meetings/WGISS-61/7.4_Phisat-2_ESA%20V3.pdf
- OPS-SAT: 18 Dec 2019 – re-entry 22/23 May 2024; 134 teams from 26 countries, >284 experiments; firsts include onboard training of ML models and re-training with live data (OrbitAI, Labrèche et al. 2022); NanoSat MO Framework. https://esa.int/Enabling_Support/Operations/Mission_complete_for_ESA_s_OPS-SAT_flying_laboratory ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPS-SAT
- HPE Spaceborne Computer-2: launched 20 Feb 2021 (NG-15), installed April 2021, returned 11 Jan 2023; upgraded unit on NG-20 (Jan 2024). EL4000 with x86 CPU + NVIDIA T4 GPU and ProLiant DL360. Genomics test Aug 2021: 182 GB raw → 13 MB downlinked (~0.007 %); ISS allotment 2 h/week, max 250 KB/s. https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/genomics-testing-on-the-iss-with-hpe-spaceborne-computer2-and-azure/ ; https://www.sdxcentral.com/news/hpe-readies-launch-of-upgraded-iss-based-spaceborne-computer-2/
- Jetson in orbit: Jetson Orin NX on Aethero cubesat, Transporter-11 (Aug 2024) with Cosmic Shielding; Aethero NxN module; GANIL heavy-ion tests (UPC/BSC with ESA) on Orin NX and Xavier NX: ~80 % of events masked as correctable SEUs, no destructive latch-up. Firefly Elytra/Ocula with Jetson on Blue Ghost M2 (NET late 2026 → 2027). https://futurumgroup.com/insights/nvidia-jetson-in-lunar-orbit-signals-commercial-gpus-are-ready-for-spaceflight/ ; https://www.space.com/ai-nvidia-gpu-spacex-launch-transporter-11
- Planet Pelican-4 onboard aircraft detection with Jetson Orin, 25 Mar 2026 (ch43 notes).
- NVIDIA announced Space-1 Vera Rubin Module at GTC (16 Mar 2026), claims up to 25× H100 AI compute; not yet available; partners incl. Axiom, Kepler, Planet, Starcloud. https://www.satellitetoday.com/technology/2026/03/17/nvidia-launches-new-chips-for-space-missions/
- Kepler optical relay first tranche: 10 satellites (~300 kg) launched 11 Jan 2026 on Falcon 9 with SDA-compatible optical terminals and multi-GPU compute modules; service launched Aug 2026; Axiom ODC nodes to fly on this network (launch not confirmed). https://kepler.space/kepler-successfully-launches-first-tranche-of-optical-relay-satellites/ ; https://www.satellitetoday.com/technology/2026/08/24/kepler-launches-service-on-its-new-optical-data-relay-constellation/

Processors
- RAD750: released 2001, first in space 2005; 110–200 MHz; ≥266 MIPS; CPU 5 W (board 10 W); TID 200 krad–1 Mrad (CPU); Curiosity, Perseverance, JWST (118 MHz). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAD750
- GR740 (Frontgrade Gaisler, ESA NGMP): quad-core LEON4FT, 250 MHz, 1 000 MIPS / 1 800 DMIPS, double-precision FPU per core, typical core power < 3 W, TID 300 krad(Si), SEL LET > 125 MeV·cm²/mg, QML-V and QML-Q (Dec 2024 brief). https://www.download.gaisler.com/products/gr740/doc/Product%20Brief_GR740_20241209.pdf
- Microchip PIC64-HPSC: announced Jul 2024 (NASA selected Microchip 2022 for ≥100× current spaceflight computing); 8 SiFive X280 RISC-V cores with vector extensions + S7 system controller; up to 2 TOPS int8 / 1 TFLOPS bfloat16 vector matrix; RH variant QML-Y, TID 100 krad (tested to 200 krad); RT variant for LEO; early-access samples 2025; secondary report (early 2026) of schedule slip; no flight known. https://www.microchip.com/en-us/about/news-releases/products/microchip-unveils-industrys-highest-performance-64-bit-hpsc-mpu ; https://eejournal.com/article/microchip-unveils-pic64-family-of-risc-v-multicore-processor-chips-for-earth-and-for-space
- AMD XQR Versal AI Core XQRVC1902: 7 nm, 400 AI engines, dual A72 + dual R5, Class B qualification completed (Dec 2022), shipping from early 2023 (Electronic Design, 2 Dec 2022). Dec 2025 AMD: enhanced lidless package seeking Class Y, sampling 2026, flight units 2027; Versal AI Edge Gen 2 XQR (commercial up to 184 TOPS) space versions 2029. Commercial VC1902 peak 133 INT8 TOPS (vendor figure). https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/embedded-revolution/article/21255763/electronic-design-programmable-ai-soc-built-to-handle-space-hazards ; https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/2025/12/amd-expands-space-grade-portfolio-enhances-in-orbit-processing-capability-and-extends-mission-timelines/
- Myriad 2: 28 nm, 12 SHAVE cores, ~0.5–1 W, vendor "TeraFLOPS within 1 W" (cores ~200 GFLOPS). Myriad X: ~1 TOPS DNN (>4 TOPS total), "10× Myriad 2 DNN performance in same power envelope". https://www.anandtech.com/show/11771 ; https://ubergizmo.com/2014/07/movidius-myriad-2
- Starcloud-1: ~60 kg (Astro Digital Corvus-Micro+ bus), launched 2 Nov 2025 on Falcon 9 Bandwagon-4, ~325 km orbit; one NVIDIA H100 (data-centre GPU rated up to 700 W); company-reported: trained nanoGPT on Shakespeare and ran inference with an open language model in orbit (announced 10 Dec 2025). Starcloud-2 (multiple GPUs incl. Blackwell, large deployable radiator) planned Oct 2026, reported Aug 2026 slipped to 2027. Raised $170 M (Mar 2026). https://www.digit.in/features/general/worlds-1st-llm-trained-in-space-from-earth-to-orbit-on-nvidia-h100-gpus.html ; https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/30/starcloud-raises-170-million-series-ato-build-data-centers-in-space ; https://spacenexus.us/orbital-data-centers/starcloud
- Google Project Suncatcher (Agüera y Arcas et al., arXiv 2511.19468, Nov 2025): Trillium (v6e) TPUs tested with 67 MeV protons at UC Davis Crocker; dawn–dusk SSO, 81-satellite cluster radius 1 km at 650 km mean altitude; shielded (10 mm Al) dose ~150 rad(Si)/yr, 5-yr ~750 rad(Si); HBM irregularities after 2 krad(Si); no hard TID failure to 15 krad(Si) on one chip; SDC in logic/SRAM ~1 per 14.4–20 rad (≈1 per 3 million inferences at 1 inference/s); HBM UECC ~1 per 44 rad (203 events); SEFI ~1 per 5 krad per chip; host CPU SEFI ~1 per 450 rad, RAM 400 rad. ISL bench 800 Gbps each way (1.6 Tbps bidirectional), ~10 Tbps per link envisaged. Launch ≲ $200/kg by mid-2030s → launched power price ~$810/kW/y (Starlink v2 mini) vs terrestrial $570–3 000/kW/y; current $3 600/kg → $14 700/kW/y. Solar panels in some orbits receive up to 8× the yearly energy of a mid-latitude panel. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19468
- Suncatcher prototype with four TPUs launched 1 Oct 2026 on Transporter-18 with Planet; one-year test (ch43 notes).
- Three-Body Computing Constellation (ADA Space + Zhejiang Lab): 12 satellites launched 14 May 2025 on Long March 2D (Jiuquan); 744 TOPS per satellite, ~5 POPS combined; laser ISLs up to 100 Gbps; 30 TB shared storage; 8-billion-parameter onboard model; planned 2 800 satellites (Star-Compute programme), target 1 000 POPS. Feb 2026 (CGTN): ~9 months of tests, 10 AI models deployed, two 8 B-parameter models (remote sensing; astronomical time-domain), 6 satellites linked; Nov 2025 survey of 189 km² in NW China; GRB classifier 99 % accuracy. No second batch found as of Oct 2026. https://starlust.org/the-three-body-computing-constellation-begins-as-china-sends-first-ai-supercomputer-satellites-to-orbit/ ; https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-02-14/China-demonstrates-AI-computing-power-in-outer-space-1KKLi5q1UPe/share_amp.html
- SpaceX FCC application (30 Jan 2026) for up to one million "Orbital Data Center" satellites, 500–2 000 km, 30° and SSO; accepted for filing 4 Feb 2026 (DA 26-113); no decision found. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-113A1.pdf ; https://satnews.com/2026/01/31/spacex-files-fcc-application-for-million-satellite-orbital-data-center/
