## Radiation
- NASA-STD-3001 Vol 1 Rev B/C: career effective dose < 600 mSv, universal for all ages and sexes (adopted 2022, based
  on NASA cancer model applied to 35-year-old female → mean 3 % REID); SPE design limit 250 mSv per event for the
  Oct 1989 design spectrum; nuclear technologies 20 mSv per mission-year. Aluminium/polyethylene shielding: ~50 %
  average SPE dose reduction but only ~7 % for GCR (OCHMO-TB-020 Rev E, 27 Dec 2022).
  https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/radiation-protection-technical-brief-ochmo.pdf ;
  https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2021/06/nasa-should-update-astronaut-radiation-exposure-limits-improve-communication-of-cancer-risks
- ISS: average effective dose ≈ 72 mSv per ~6-month mission (19 astronauts, Cucinotta et al. 2008, Radiation Research);
  roughly 80–160 mSv per six months between solar max and min (SIDC/STCE summary of NASA figures).
- Scott Kelly (Twins Study, 340 days): absorbed 76.18 mGy, effective dose 146.34 mSv (PMC6711946 review of Science 2019).
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6711946/
- Chang'e-4 LND: lunar surface dose equivalent 1 369 µSv/day (Zhang et al. 2020, Science Advances); ISS in same period
  731 µSv/day. https://www.sci.news/space/radiation-dose-rates-lunar-surface-08893.html
- MSL RAD: cruise ≈ 1.84 mSv/day (Zeitlin et al. 2013, Science); Mars surface 0.64 mSv/day (Hassler et al. 2014,
  Science); ~1 Sv for a 180+500+180-day mission. (Long-established values.)
- Artemis I MARE (Nature 634, 2024; DLR 18 Sep 2024): factor-4 dose-rate difference between least and most shielded
  locations in the inner belt; 90° attitude change cut belt dose rate by 50 %; shelter location could limit large-SPE
  dose to ≤ 150 mSv. https://www.dlr.de/en/latest/news/2024/orion-spacecraft-radiation-protection-tested-initial-findings-from-artemis-i-moon-mission
- Artemis II (Apr 2026) tested the storm-shelter procedure; no published crew dose numbers found (2026-10-18).

## Atmosphere and standards
- NASA-STD-3001 Vol 2 Rev F approved 2026-07-14 (supersedes Rev E). Key values: ≥ 30 % diluent [V2 6002]; PIO2 =
  (PB − 47 mmHg) × FIO2; normoxia 145–155 mmHg; indefinite hyperoxia limit 356 mmHg; mild-hypoxia lower limit
  127 mmHg (1-h TWA; absolute 122); 1-h average ppCO2 ≤ 3 mmHg [V2 6004]; total pressure 34.5 < p ≤ 103 kPa [V2 6006];
  ≤ 13.5 psi/min pressure change. Exploration atmosphere example 8.2 psia / 34 % O2.
  https://standards.nasa.gov/system/files/tmp/NASA-STD-3001%20Vol%202%20Rev%20F.pdf
- NASA-STD-3001 Vol 1 Rev C approved 2023-09-15. Human-rating: NPR 8705.2C (process) with technical requirements
  moved to NASA-STD-8719.29 (baseline 2023-12-12).
  https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/C//nasa-std-3001-vol-1-rev-c-signature.pdf ;
  https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/NASA/Baseline/0/NASA-STD-871929-Baseline.pdf
- Exploration atmosphere: EAWG 8.0 psia/32 % (2005) → 8.2 psia (56.5 kPa)/34 % O2 (2012); 2024 NASA M2M presentation:
  specifications "still being evaluated"; 2026 NASA study (Abney et al., May 2026): 9 subsystems viable at 10.2 psia,
  only 3 at 8.2 psia; flammability the critical hurdle at 34–40 % O2 (~70 % of subsystems need material changes);
  2026 ICES textiles paper: certify textiles to 37 % O2 (34 % + 3 % margin). 31.4 % O2 = calculated limit for no
  increase in burning rate at 56.5 kPa (AMHP 2018).
  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260003925 ; https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240003211 ;
  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260003042/downloads/301-ICES_Paper_2026-FR-Textiles_Final_04-13-2026.pdf
- ISS CO2: 2001–2012 typically 2.3–5.3 mmHg; headache odds double per +1 mmHg; keep 7-day mean < 2.5 mmHg for < 1 %
  headache risk (OCHMO CO2 technical brief). https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-004-carbon-dioxide.pdf

## Metabolic budget and ECLSS
- BVAD Rev 2 (NASA/TP-2015-218570/REV2, Feb 2022), Table 3-31, 82 kg crew member, RQ 0.86, 90 min/day exercise:
  O2 0.895 kg/d; CO2 1.085 kg/d; food solids 0.80 kg/d; potable water 3.217 kg/d; water in food 0.76; metabolic water
  0.49; heat 12.43 MJ/d (6.31 sensible, 6.12 latent); respiration+perspiration water 2.946; urine water 1.42; urine
  solids 0.061; faecal water 0.101; faecal solids 0.032; sweat/misc solids 0.027. ISS food 1.83 → 2.39 kg/CM-d
  incl. packaging. Transit-vehicle water 3.2 kg/CM-d (Table 4-20). WPA expendables ≈ 472 kg/yr. UPA recovers ≈ 85 %
  (US) / 70 % (Russian) of urine water. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210024855
- Older design values: O2 0.84, CO2 1.00 kg/CM-d (Weiland 1994, used by Jones 2017).
- Jones, ICES-2017-85: ISS ECLSS masses (4 crew) — OGS 676 kg (+399 spares set), CDRA 195 (+156), CO2 reduction 329
  (+219), WRS 1 383 (+719); Shuttle LiOH canister 7 kg for 4 crew-days (1.75 kg/CM-d).
  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20170007268
- ISS 98 % total water recovery demonstrated with the Brine Processor Assembly (NASA, June 2023; previously 93–94 %).
  https://space.com/astronaut-pee-iss-water-recycling-98-percent-milestone
- Four Bed CO2 Scrubber: launched Aug 2021, activated Oct 2021, operating as a primary CO2 removal system (ICES 2024).
  https://ttu-ir.tdl.org/items/f90686f4-a3f2-4778-bb8a-ba2dcc430cd3
- Sabatier: flight-proven on ISS; O2 recovery limited to ~50 % because half the H2 leaves as methane (ICES 2024 Paper 168;
  ICES 2026-190). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240002161
- Crew Dragon air revitalisation: LiOH sheets, activated charcoal, HEPA, ammonia-removal resin, Nafion humidity
  control (ICES-2020-333 via Aerospace America). Orion: CAMRAS amine swing bed (NASA).
- Soyuz: KO2 regenerator, LiOH absorbs part (~20 %) of CO2 (govt technical document; hedge).
- Saffire series 2016–2024; Saffire-VI ended 9 Jan 2024, tested 41 cm-wide samples at reduced pressure / raised O2.
  https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/flame-burns-out-on-nasas-long-running-spacecraft-fire-experiment/

## Health, analogues, AI
- SANS prevalence: meta-analysis optic disc oedema ~75 %, choroidal folds ~71 %; prospective ISS cohort 69 % ODE on OCT
  by flight day 150. (Search summary of 2023–26 literature; hedge "most long-duration crew show some signs".)
- SOMA / Inspiration4 (Nature, June 2024): 44 papers; short flight caused broad molecular changes similar to long
  missions (telomere elongation, cytokines); most returned to baseline within months.
- CHAPEA: Mission 1 25 Jun 2023 – 6 Jul 2024 (378 days); Mission 2 began 19 Oct 2025, planned end 31 Oct 2026,
  ~1 700 ft² 3D-printed habitat at JSC. https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/chapea-crew-begins-stay-inside-nasas-mars-habitat-for-second-mission
- Lunar Palace 1 (Yuegong-1): 370-day test May 2017 – May 2018, ~98 % self-sufficient (Beihang/Xinhua).
- BIOS-3: 180-day 3-person closure 1972–73, 315 m³.
- MELiSSA pilot plant (UAB Barcelona): long-term operation; ESA strategy 2025–2040 (May 2025).
- CMO-DA (NASA + Google, 2025): AI clinical decision support prototype; diagnostic accuracy 88 % (ankle), 80 % (ear),
  74 % (flank pain) in simulated cases. https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/public-sector/how-google-and-nasa-are-testing-ai-for-medical-care-in-space
- AI for ECLSS: supervised anomaly detection/diagnosis toolchain for Columbus ECLSS (CEAS Space Journal, Aug 2025,
  doi 10.1007/s12567-025-00654-3).

## Risk
- Commercial Crew: LOC ≤ 1 in 270 for a mission up to 210 days; ascent and entry 1 in 500 (program statements
  2011–2018). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human-rating_certification
- Shuttle retrospective: STS-1 ≈ 1-in-12 LOC; ≈ 1 in 90 by 2011 (NASA statements reported by NBC/Space Review).
